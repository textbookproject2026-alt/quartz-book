# Commenting in the Margins

**A guide for students**

Every page of this textbook has a margin you can write in. You can ask a question
about a sentence you didn't follow, point out a typo, or argue with a claim —
and it stays attached to the exact words you were reading.

This page explains how to read what's already in the margins, how to add your
own, and two things about it that surprise people. It takes five minutes.

---

## Reading the comments — nothing needed

Anyone can read the margin comments. No account, no sign-up, no login.

Under the title of every chapter there's a row of small links, and at the end of
it a badge. It says **"3 annotations"** if there are three, or **"Annotate this
page"** if there are none yet.

Click the badge. A sidebar opens along the right-hand edge of the page, listing
every comment on that chapter. Click one and the passage it belongs to lights up
in the text, so you can see what the person was actually talking about.

You can also just start reading and watch for highlighted passages — those are
places someone has already said something.

Close the sidebar with the same badge, or the arrow at its top corner.

---

## Leaving your own comment

For this you need a free account with **Hypothes.is**, the service that runs the
margin. It takes about a minute.

1. Go to **https://hypothes.is** and sign up. It's free and it asks for an email
   address and a username — nothing else.
2. Come back to the textbook and open the sidebar (the badge under the chapter
   title).
3. **Log in** at the top of the sidebar.

Pick your username with a little care: **it appears next to every comment you
leave, and anyone can see it.** Plenty of people use something that isn't their
full name. That's fine.

Then, to comment:

1. **Select the text you want to comment on** — drag across a sentence, a phrase,
   a whole paragraph, whatever the comment is about.
2. A small **Annotate** button appears next to what you selected. Click it.
3. The sidebar opens with an empty comment box, already attached to your
   selection. Type, then click **Post**.

That's it. Your comment is now in the margin of that chapter, anchored to those
exact words, and it stays there.

You can edit or delete anything you've posted — the **⋮** menu at the top-right
of your own comment. You can't edit or delete anyone else's, and they can't touch
yours.

**Replying** works the same way: open the sidebar, find a comment, click
**Reply**. Most of the good conversations on the page are replies rather than new
comments.

## Two things people don't expect

### Your comments are public

They are not private to your class, and they are not visible only to your tutor.
A comment you post here can be read by anyone on the internet, whether or not
they have an account — the same way you can read everyone else's without one.

The textbook does not run separate comment spaces for separate classes. That was
considered and decided against, so it isn't coming later either. There is one
margin, and everybody shares it.

None of that is a reason not to comment. It just means: write what you'd be happy
to have your name on. If you want to say something you'd rather keep between you
and your tutor, use whatever your course normally uses for that — email, the
course forum — not the margin.

> [!warning] Don't put personal information in a comment
> Not your student number, not your email address, not anything about your
> health, your marks, or anyone else. Once it's posted it's on the open web.

### Last year's comments are already there

The margin isn't wiped between cohorts. Open a chapter and you may find comments
from students who took the course a year or two ago, sitting alongside this
year's.

This is deliberate — a good question from two years ago is still a good question,
and the answers under it are worth reading. But it does mean the sidebar isn't a
picture of what _your_ class is currently discussing, and a comment with no reply
may simply be old rather than ignored.

Each comment shows the date it was posted, under the username. Check it if you're
unsure whether a conversation is live.

## Tags (optional, and often skipped)

Comments here come in roughly two kinds, and there are two words for them:

- **`copy-edit`** — you've spotted a mistake. A typo, a broken link, a wrong
  number, a sentence that doesn't parse.
- **`discussion`** — a question, a disagreement, an idea. Nothing is broken; you
  want to talk about it.

Adding one of these words as a **tag** helps whoever is looking after the book
sort corrections from conversation. It's genuinely useful, and it's also
genuinely optional.

While the sidebar is open, a small panel appears just to its left, with both
words on it as buttons. Here's what it actually does, because it looks like it
should do more:

**Clicking one copies that word to your clipboard.** That's all. You then paste
it into the **Tags** field underneath your comment before you post.

It can't fill the field in for you. The comment box lives inside a frame the
textbook's own page isn't allowed to write into — a browser security rule, not an
oversight — so copy-and-paste is as close as it can get.

So: **most comments have no tag at all, and that's completely fine.** An untagged
comment is read the same as a tagged one. If you're reporting an obvious typo,
the tag saves someone a moment; otherwise, don't think about it.

## If something doesn't work

**No badge under the chapter title.** The page couldn't reach Hypothes.is.
Usually a browser extension — an ad-blocker or a privacy blocker — or a network
that blocks it. Try a different browser, or your phone off wi-fi.

**The badge says "annotation tools blocked".** Same cause, and now the page is
telling you outright. Same fixes.

**You selected text and no Annotate button appeared.** Try selecting a bit more —
a very short selection can miss. Selections that cross an image or a table
sometimes don't take either.

**Your comment vanished after the chapter was edited.** It hasn't been deleted.
When the wording a comment was attached to changes a lot, the comment can't find
its place any more, so it moves to an **Orphans** tab at the top of the sidebar.
It's still there, still yours, still readable.

**The comment is on the wrong sentence.** Occasionally an edit shifts a highlight
onto a similar phrase elsewhere on the page. Annoying, harmless, nothing you did.

If none of that helps, tell your course coordinator rather than trying to fix it —
it's almost always something at the site's end.

## The short version

- Reading comments: click the badge under the chapter title. No account.
- Writing them: free Hypothes.is account, select text, click **Annotate**, type,
  **Post**.
- Everything you post is public and shows your username.
- Old comments from previous years stay on the page. That's normal.
- Tags are nice, optional, and copy-paste only.
